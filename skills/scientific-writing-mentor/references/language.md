# Scientific language and cohesion

## Information flow

Connect a sentence opening to information the reader can identify. Give important new information emphasis, often near the end, and carry it forward through an explicit noun or clearly resolved reference. A bare "this" may need "this decrease" or "this comparison" when its referent is ambiguous.

Keep one technical name for one object. Cycling among "membrane", "platform", "device" and "technology" for variety can alter the referent. Keep the subject and governing verb close enough to follow; move long conditions where they preserve scope. Put actions in verbs when nominalizations hide what happened.

## Tense expresses function

- Established knowledge and what a display shows often use the present.
- Completed procedures and study-specific observations often use the past.
- Work extending to the current state of the field can justify the present perfect.
- Planned work remains distinguishable from completed research.

Choose from meaning and current field convention. A tense change can turn a historical observation into a general fact or change whether a gap remains current. Do not impose one tense on an entire section.

## Voice, rhythm and modality

Use active voice when the actor matters, passive when the process or object should be foregrounded. Do not invent an actor or ban all passives. Prefer one concise sentence when it states the same point clearly; split only when distinct inferences or competing conditions need the separation. Preserve readable rhythm and scope rather than maximizing sentence density or following a fixed length. Apply the author's [advisor priorities](advisor-priorities.md) to focus, paragraph transitions and Results openings and conclusions.

Use the strongest wording supported by the design. A measured difference, association, plausible mechanism and causal intervention justify different verbs. Compress duplicated hedging while preserving distinct uncertainties. A non-significant finding does not establish equivalence.

## Defensive or mechanical prose

Find repeated answers to imaginary objections, empty contrasts, broad disclaimers, inflated significance and repeated conclusions. State the supported relation and its actual condition. Preserve meaningful contrasts, negative findings and necessary limitations. Avoid technical-looking labels with no established meaning and forced casualization.

## Chinese-to-English revision

Recover the intended logical relation before translating sentence by sentence. Preserve terminology, polarity, baselines, units, quantities and hedging. Explain consequential restructuring outside the paper. Do not add a mechanism or citation absent from the source merely to make the English sound complete.

## Fidelity check

Compare values, units, names, references, negation, quantifiers, baselines, causal verbs, tense and scope. Words and punctuation are diagnostic clues, not a blacklist or proof of AI authorship.
