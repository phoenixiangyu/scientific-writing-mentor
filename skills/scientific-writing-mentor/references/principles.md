# Reader-oriented scientific writing

These are adaptable heuristics; see [source-map.md](source-map.md) for provenance.

## Scientific question before activity

Separate the practical setting from what the reader learns. A device, installation or synthesis is a setting; a tested relation, mechanism, capability or trade-off can be the scientific contribution. Find that contribution in the actual results. A bounded measurement, useful negative finding or resource can be valuable without a claim of universal superiority.

## Give information a function

Identify what each paragraph enables the reader to understand: context, uncertainty, question, test, finding, explanation or boundary. Describe what a paragraph accomplishes, not just its topic. Arrange it around the inference, not the dates on which experiments were performed.

## Meet the intended reader

Supply the context needed for the next inference. Specialists and multidisciplinary readers need different starting points. Avoid unexplained technical jumps and unnecessary textbook background. Reassess the opening and framing when transferring journals.

Study recent comparable papers to identify rhetorical functions and audience conventions. Write original prose about the user's work; do not borrow model-paper data, conclusions or distinctive sentences.

## Plan and update

Prepare an outline while the work develops and update it when observations change. A planned narrative is provisional, not an instruction to select only favorable results. Diagnose unsupported claims, missing transitions and wrong section functions before polishing words.

## Scientific confidence

State supported findings directly. Replace generic apology with a concrete condition or boundary. Retain a negative finding, unfavorable comparison or failure condition when it changes the interpretation. Put requests for missing evidence in comments; keep substantive limitations where readers need them in the manuscript.

## Figures and captions

Give a figure a scientific question and a supported answer. Group panels that establish that comparison. Align legends, units and numbers with the prose. Mention the figure near the finding without reading out every value. Caption capitalization, paragraph length and display budgets remain venue-dependent rather than universal teaching rules.
