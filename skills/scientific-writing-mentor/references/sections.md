# Section functions

Use the relevant part only. Functions below are not compulsory paragraph counts.

## Title

Identify the scientific object and central relationship, capability or finding. Match specificity to evidence and audience. Check the title against the actual results, abstract and conclusion. A working title naming an activity may need revision once the contribution is known.

## Abstract / summary

Orient the reader, identify the particular question, state the approach at a useful level, report decisive findings and explain bounded significance. Select numbers that carry the argument and identify their comparison and units. Avoid crowding out the contribution with background or a catalogue of achievements.

Use the actual venue/article format. A research abstract, review abstract, structured abstract and Nature summary paragraph have different functions and constraints. Reconcile the abstract with the finished paper.

## Introduction

Move from an audience-appropriate entry point to a specific uncertainty. Explain what is known, what the relevant literature leaves unresolved, why it matters, and the study's aim and approach. Organize literature by the argument rather than author-by-author summaries.

Show importance through scientific consequences. A specialist audience may need little broad background. "Few studies exist" is useful only when verified and connected to an identifiable knowledge gap. End with the contribution at the level the journal expects.

## Methods / experimental / modelling

Orient before procedural detail. Organize by the design and analyses required to interpret and reproduce the findings. Explain relevant modifications and non-obvious choices. Retain necessary sampling units, controls, exclusions, conditions, parameters, software and analysis assumptions.

Never complete missing protocols, replicate counts, parameters or approvals from plausibility. Record requests in comments. Check whether the venue places specific detail in the main paper, Methods or SI.

## Results

Lead a unit with the scientific question or finding, give the essential comparison and evidence, then the supported local inference. Include necessary magnitude, uncertainty, denominators and statistics without narrating every table value.

Brief methodological orientation can be needed for interpretation; do not duplicate a procedure mechanically. Local interpretation in Results is not universally forbidden. Choose separate or combined Results and Discussion from article function, venue and logical dependencies.

## Discussion

Anchor the reader in the important answer, then widen toward its contribution to knowledge. Synthesize across results, compare with prior work, and distinguish demonstrated explanations from plausible alternatives. Avoid replaying every figure.

State limitations through their consequence for a specific claim or its transfer. Link future work to a named unresolved question. Calibrate verbs and modality instead of attaching caution to every sentence.

## Conclusion

Give the supported answer and its contribution at the appropriate level of generality. Do not duplicate the abstract, introduce new results or expand the claim beyond its evidence. Include implications or a remaining question only when useful and appropriate to the venue.

## Captions and SI

Make displays interpretable: sample identity, conditions, symbols, units, uncertainty and relevant statistics. Keep all values consistent with the text. Route supporting detail to SI only when it preserves the main inference and meets venue rules.
