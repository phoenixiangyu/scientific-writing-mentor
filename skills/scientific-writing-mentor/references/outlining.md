# Outlining and structural revision

## New outline

1. State the research question and strongest supported answer. Identify planned work as planned in the working notes.
2. List essential findings and their source figures, tables, analyses or author notes. Track hypotheses separately.
3. Order the findings by what the reader needs to understand first. Identify logical dependencies.
4. Develop a figure sequence alongside the outline; check that each panel can support the proposed finding.
5. Give proposed paragraphs a function, claim and evidence source. Put missing-data requests in the accompanying issue list.
6. Revisit title, abstract and introduction after the evidence sequence is stable. The final framing follows the actual results.

An optional planning table:

| Section / paragraph | Reader question | Supported point | Evidence source | Display item | Open issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Results 1 | What changed under the tested condition? | Supplied finding | Author analysis | Figure 1 | Off-text verification question |

This is a working artifact, not manuscript text. Do not force a Theory section, separate Conclusion, or fixed paragraph count where the article type does not call for it.

## Reverse outline

Summarize each paragraph's function in one line. Find duplicate functions, combined unrelated inferences, evidence introduced before its question, or conclusions repeated without new support. Reorder, merge, split or remove within the authorized editing scope.

Check both directions: central claims need support; retained experiments need a scientific purpose. Keep disconfirming experiments important to the interpretation.

## Missing experiments

Explain which inference needs a test and what observation would distinguish the alternatives. Put the plan in comments or planning notes. Offer a narrower evidence-supported paper when appropriate. Do not invent expected results or require a new experimental campaign before a local language edit.
